Black Holes

A black hole is a region in space where matter is packed so incredibly tight that its gravitational pull becomes an inescapable cosmic trap. They usually form when massive stars collapse under their own weight at the end of their lives, creating a point of infinite density known as a singularity. Surrounding this point is an invisible boundary called the event horizon—the ultimate point of no return. Because the gravity inside this boundary is so intense that nothing, not even light, can escape its grip, black holes remain completely invisible to our eyes, revealing themselves only when they warp the space around them or violently shred and consume nearby stars.
